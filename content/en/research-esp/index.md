When free gas enters an electrical submersible pump (ESP), the boosting pressure degrades, then surges, and may end in gas lock. The group studies the chain from bubble breakup and coalescence in the rotating impeller to in-situ gas void fraction, flow-pattern transition and boosting degradation, extending to high-viscosity, emulsion and sand-laden conditions.

{{< fig src="uploads/research/esp-surging-curves.jpg" alt="Boosting degradation and surging of an ESP under gassy conditions: experiment vs. mechanistic model" caption="Boosting degradation and surging of an ESP under gassy conditions: experiment vs. mechanistic model" >}}

Work combines the TUALP multistage two-phase ESP loop, high-speed imaging inside rotating impellers and CFD, leading to mechanistic boosting models, inflection-point–based flow-pattern identification from performance curves, hydraulic design and automated CFD optimization of wide-range offshore ESP impellers and diffusers, and data-driven condition diagnosis.

Representative publications are listed on the [Chinese page]({{< u "research/esp/" >}}) and in [Outputs]({{< u "en/outputs/" >}}); see also [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=sfsM2TUAAAAJ&hl=en).
