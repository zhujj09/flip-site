Gas–liquid flow from the bottom hole to the surface passes through vertical, deviated and horizontal sections, with stratified, slug, churn and annular patterns whose pressure-drop and holdup behavior differ widely. Wellbore multiphase flow models are the common basis for artificial-lift design, liquid-loading diagnosis and production optimization; the group develops both steady-state and transient models.

{{< fig src="uploads/research/mp-transient-model.jpg" alt="Transient three-phase flow model compared with OLGA" caption="Transient three-phase flow model compared with OLGA" >}}

{{< fig src="uploads/research/mp-unified-model.jpg" alt="Unified slug-unit model and flow-pattern verification" caption="Unified multiphase model: slug-unit control volume and flow-pattern boundary verification" >}}

**Steady state.** A unified model built on the slug-unit control volume covers all inclinations and oil–gas–water three-phase flow, with improved closure relations for interfacial friction, emulsion rheology, slug holdup and translational velocity. It serves as the wellbore backbone of the plunger-lift, ESP and jet-pump models.

**Transient and PINN.** A transient drift-flux model for horizontal gas wells is benchmarked against OLGA, and physics-informed neural networks (PI-DeepONet, time-block XPINN) are used for fast computation. Liquid-loading onset is predicted with clustering and convolutional neural networks.
